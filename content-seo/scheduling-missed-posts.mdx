A blog's **scheduled date** places it in the content calendar and helps determine when it is eligible for generation. Its **status** tells you which stage it has reached. Check both before moving a date, requesting generation, or assuming a page should already be live.

The generation date is separate from the publishing queue's timestamp and the actual website's result. Choosing a date is not a guarantee of a finished article or publication that day.

## How the scheduled date drives your calendar

Open the selected project's **Content SEO** dashboard and inspect the idea's date and state. The normal automatic scan selects ideas dated for the server's current day and still marked **To be generated**, with completed planning and the other eligibility conditions in place.

A future-dated idea awaiting its first generation remains **To be generated**. **Scheduled** is a different workflow state associated with publishing; do not use it as a general label for every dated idea.

The current scheduler is configured around UTC, while a date picker can use your local day. Near midnight, inspect the date actually saved. For other conditions affecting selection, see [Automated Generation](/content-seo/automated-generation).

## What "Missed" means

The missed-post sweep looks for unclaimed **To be generated** ideas whose date has passed and changes qualifying ones to **Missed**. The detail view labels this **Schedule Missed**. The normal generation scan does not automatically revive that state.

Missed tells you that the idea needs attention; it does not independently establish a charge, refund, or quota outcome. Check [Quota & Limits](/content-seo/quota-and-limits) when interpreting usage. The sweep also has a workload bound, so an overdue idea can remain To be generated until it is processed.

<h3 id="missing-a-day-or-two-is-fine">Decide which missed topics to recover</h3>

Review whether the topic is still useful before catching up. A promotion that has ended may need a new angle; a useful evergreen explanation may still be worth generating. These are illustrative editorial decisions, not evidence that a particular posting total determines SEO results.

Choose recovery dates and review responsibilities based on what the team can finish well. Generating several drafts together does not promise that search performance will compensate for a missed day.

## Recovering a missed post

1. Open the idea from the selected project's calendar or blog list.
2. Inspect its **Schedule Missed** state, topic, and saved plan.
3. Use **Edit Plan** or **Regenerate plan** when available if the topic needs work.
4. When ready, use the manual generation action. The pre-generation plan card labels it **Generate Blog**; another status-action view can label it **Generate Content**.
5. Inspect the resulting status and any error. A started request is not a completed article.

Generation still needs eligible access, quota, and a launchable state. If blocked, resolve the reported cause before retrying. Do not assume a Missed post bypasses these checks.

Changing the date alone leaves a Missed idea in that state. Use its available generation action when you want the content created. Regenerating the plan changes the topic plan rather than serving as proof that the article body was generated.

## Bonus blogs that are "awaiting a date"

Bonus ideas added through **Generate More** begin in **Awaiting date**. They stay outside normal automatic generation until you successfully commit a date. The system can store a placeholder date without treating that idea as ready to run.

In the dashboard's bonus **Pick a date** panel, choose today or a future date and click **Set date**. On success, the idea becomes **To be generated** and joins the regular calendar. Inspect the saved date, especially near a timezone boundary.

If its topic planning was incomplete, setting a date can attempt that planning first. A failure can leave the idea in Awaiting date instead of activating it. Read the error; see [Generate More & Bonus Blogs](/content-seo/generate-more-bonus-blogs) for recovery.

The bonus date picker does not limit you to the current billing window. A saved future date still does not establish future access or promise that generation will run then.

## Changing a scheduled date

For an idea showing the editable pre-generation plan card:

1. Open the blog and choose **Edit Plan**.
2. Change **Scheduled Date** and inspect the other plan fields.
3. Click **Save Changes**.
4. Reopen or inspect the idea to confirm the saved date and status.

The editing controls are disabled while generation or plan regeneration is in progress. Do not treat an accepted date change as a reschedule of a completed CMS publication, a cancellation of a queued operation, or a reversal of already recorded generation usage.

The bonus **Set date** action and **Edit Plan** use different paths. The former validates an awaiting idea and changes its state on success; a regular date edit does not automatically move Missed back into the generation queue. If the desired control is absent, inspect the current state rather than assuming every blog supports the same scheduling action.
