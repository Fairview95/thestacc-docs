theStacc uses an Article Length target during generation and shows a word count with the finished post. Use that count to review the draft alongside its content and SEO suggestions. A useful article answers the reader's question; adding words alone does not make the answer better.

## How length is decided

Choose **Article Length** in the Writing tab of [Preferences](/content-seo/preferences), considering the topic and [blog type](/content-seo/blog-types). The saved choice supplies a target range to the writer during generation, rather than imposing an exact final word count.

A broad guide may need more explanation than a focused how-to. The useful distinction is what each post needs to cover: prerequisites, steps, examples, alternatives, or limitations. Review those needs before deciding that a draft should be longer.

The current **Article Length** options are **Concise**, **Standard** and **In-depth**. They request different target ranges for generation; you still review the finished article before publishing.

## Where to check the word count

Open the **blog detail page** to review the word count displayed with the SEO score. It describes the generated post's text, rather than the target used when planning it.

Use it to spot a draft that needs closer review, then read the relevant sections. A word count cannot tell you whether an explanation is accurate, whether important steps are missing, or whether the same advice appears several times.

If you edit an article in your website's CMS after publishing, check that version separately. Do not assume the count shown in theStacc reflects changes made outside theStacc.

## What a length suggestion means

The SEO analysis includes **content length and depth** among its checks. Read the suggestion shown for your post to understand which part needs attention. The product's scoring rules are editing guidance, not Google's ranking requirements.

Google's [people-first content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content#avoid) says Google has no preferred word count. Do not treat a numerical length recommendation as a requirement to add filler or as a promise of better rankings.

For a breakdown of the product's checks, see [SEO Scoring Explained](/content-seo/seo-scoring). Review the underlying suggestion as well as the headline score. A draft may need a clearer structure or a missing explanation rather than more words throughout.

> **Review for completeness:** A shorter draft is not automatically thin, and a longer draft is not automatically thorough. Check what the intended reader can understand or do after reading it.

## Decide whether to expand, shorten, or keep a draft

Use the reader's task to choose the next edit. These are editorial decisions, not scoring thresholds.

Before publishing:

- Confirm that the title's promise is answered in the body.
- Check prerequisites, important steps, examples, and limitations where the topic needs them.
- Verify factual claims and business details added during expansion.
- Remove repeated explanations and unrelated material.
- Use headings that describe the sections, rather than adding headings to meet a quota.

## Change an existing article

Edit the draft directly when you know what needs to change. If you use [section regeneration](/content-seo/regenerating-content#regenerate-a-section), its length control can request a shorter or longer rewrite of the selected section. Review the result for accuracy and relevance before publishing.

Save any wording you want to keep before regenerating: the [regeneration guide](/content-seo/regenerating-content#before-you-start) explains that regenerated content replaces the current version.

**Generate More** creates additional blog plans. It does not expand the article you are reviewing. See [Generate More & Bonus Blogs](/content-seo/generate-more-bonus-blogs) if you need more posts rather than a longer existing post.
