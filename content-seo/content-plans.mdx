Content Plans & Keywords connects your topic pool with the blogs planned for your project. Open **Content Plan** to review dates, titles, types and keywords before writing starts. Keep the pool useful, inspect changed rows after saving or refreshing them, and check generation and publishing separately from the plan.

## How content plans work

A content plan groups dated blog ideas. The current plan setup uses a **30-day window**, but the number and spacing of its slots can depend on the project's publishing cadence. The legacy schedule uses 30 consecutive daily placeholders. Read the actual dates in your calendar rather than assuming every project has one article per day.

A placeholder means topic planning is still pending. Filling it supplies a title, keyword, format and description; it does not mean the article body has been written. Check the completed titles and descriptions before starting article generation.

Choose the correct project, then select **Content Plan** in the sidebar. The table shows **Date**, **Blog**, **Type** and **Keyword**. Use the month arrows to inspect another month, and open a row to review the post's detail page.

### Editing a planned post

In the Content Plan table, the **Edit plan** pencil is available to project editors for a row marked **To be generated**. You can change its date, title, type and focus keyword. Other post states can offer different actions on the detail page; absence of the table pencil does not describe every editing action in the product.

1. Select the pencil on the intended row.
2. Review the date, title, type and keyword together.
3. Make the change, then select **Save**.
4. Inspect the saved row and its description. Use **Cancel** to leave the unsaved edit.

Changing a title or keyword also requests a matching description. That refresh is best effort: the main edits can save even if the AI refresh is skipped, fails or times out. Check the description yourself, including after a **Plan updated** confirmation. A date-only change does not request a new description.

### Regenerating stale plans

When business information changes, a banner can flag plans that use older context. Review the changed business details and the affected rows before choosing a refresh. The available banner actions have different effects.

The banner's **Regenerate titles** action keeps existing focus keywords. **Full refresh (new keywords)** also replaces those keywords, so read the confirmation before proceeding. Both work on eligible completed topic plans in the latest content plan. Refreshing a missed row does not change its date or move it back into the automatic generation queue.

For a smaller change, select rows and use **Regenerate … blog plans** in the floating toolbar. Inspect the result: some rows can be skipped or fail. A **Rejected** row may be selectable in this view, but the current bulk refresh accepts completed plans marked **To be generated** or **Missed**. Open a skipped post's detail page to check its individual plan actions.

For partial failures, use **View details** to inspect the affected slots and their messages. The dialog can offer **Retry** for an individual slot. Check the next result rather than assuming the retry succeeded. AI refresh actions require an active blog plan; follow an access or subscription error before trying again.

### Creating the next plan

Navigate to an empty month after the latest plan's end date. When eligible, the page offers **Generate Next Month's Plan**; an inactive plan shows an upgrade action instead. Creating another plan requires editor access and an active blog subscription.

The next window starts after the previous plan ends, or from the current day if that plan has already expired. It is another 30-day window, rather than necessarily the next calendar month. Previous keywords are passed into planning to help avoid repetition, but inspect the returned topics for overlapping intent.

Check the displayed dates and completed topic rows after generation. A progress counter reaching its end does not prove every article body exists or that anything has been published. See [Blog Generation](/content-seo/blog-generation) for the separate writing step.

## Managing your keyword pool

Select **Keywords** in the Content sidebar to manage the pool. Add topics your business can explain accurately, then inspect the planned posts they produce. Adding a phrase is not a reservation for a particular date or a promise of search traffic.

### What each keyword shows

The table shows **Keyword**, **Status**, **Used**, **Last used** and **Enabled**. The star marks **Always-On** priority. Read usage separately from publication: a keyword can have generated content that still needs review. Check its linked post when the badge or usage looks different from what you expected.

### Keyword lifecycle badges

- **Active:** associated with a scheduled or in-progress post.
- **Queued:** enabled and available for selection.
- **Cooling:** resting after recorded use.
- **Off:** disabled for rotation.

These badges describe pool state, rather than guaranteeing the next publishing action. Always-On is a separate priority flag; a starred keyword can still be occupied by a post. See [Keyword Strategy & Rotation](/content-seo/keyword-strategy-rotation) for eligibility and cooldown details.

### Adding, generating, enabling, and renaming keywords

Paste phrases one per line or separated by commas, then select **Add to pool**. Keep each phrase within **80 characters**. The input parser trims and lowercases entries, removes duplicates from the pasted list, and omits empty or overlong phrases. Check the response for added entries, existing keywords, items left in the pool and failures.

Manual additions are subject to pool capacity checks. If a limit blocks an addition, follow the returned message instead of assuming unlimited keywords can be stored. Disabling a keyword excludes it from selection; deleting it is a separate action. Check any associated article before removing a topic.

**Generate keywords** requests an AI-suggested batch. Inspect the topics and the displayed number added and generation requests left that month. A request can return fewer usable additions than you expected; duplicates and unsuccessful saves are not new pool entries.

Use search and status filters to find a group, then select rows to **Enable**, **Disable** or **Delete**. Keyword selection can cover the filtered group across pages. Individual actions can fail, so check the summary and follow any locked or in-use message. Renaming also depends on the linked post's state; use the returned error to decide whether the post needs attention first.

### Always-On keywords (priority)

Star an important keyword to make it **Always-On**. Priority puts it ahead of other eligible keywords and exempts it from the configured cooldown. It still cannot bypass a post already occupying that keyword.

Use priority for a topic with a continuing purpose. Before commissioning another article, check whether an existing page already answers the same reader question. Treat the interface's monthly repetition suggestion as guidance, rather than a verified Google allowance or ranking threshold.

## How rotation fills the calendar

Selection looks for an enabled keyword that is free from an occupying post and eligible under the cooldown rules. Among eligible choices, it prefers Always-On, then unused keywords, then the oldest recorded use, with earlier additions breaking a tie. This order helps planning select a topic; it does not guarantee which finished article you will receive.

A keyword can be unavailable because it is disabled, cooling or already occupied by a post. Keyword selection can also fail. Check the pool and the actual planned rows before generating articles; selection alone does not establish that every calendar slot has been filled.

## A note on volume, difficulty, and relevance

The Keywords table focuses on pool status and usage. A suggested phrase is not, by itself, evidence of search demand or ranking difficulty. Review customer questions, the services you actually offer and existing pages before accepting a topic. Use measured search data when that information is needed for the decision.

For detailed topic selection and the limits of generated suggestions, use [Keyword Strategy & Rotation](/content-seo/keyword-strategy-rotation). Avoid turning an AI suggestion into a forecast of traffic or a promise that a long phrase will rank quickly.

## Promotions

The dedicated Promotions Calendar is retired in the current Content SEO interface. Older Promotions settings links lead to business information rather than a monthly event-generation screen. Use the current plan and per-post direction workflow for seasonal topics; see [Promotions Calendar](/content-seo/promotions-calendar) for the change and the available alternatives.

## What happens after a plan is set

Review the planned topic before starting article generation. The saved date and completed plan are inputs to later workflow checks, not proof that the post will be written or published on that day. See [Scheduling & Missed Posts](/content-seo/scheduling-missed-posts) when a date has passed.

Check the project's [publishing mode](/content-seo/publishing-modes) and destination separately. If you need human review before a post goes live, confirm the review-first setup before relying on an approval queue. A planned calendar row alone does not establish that review is required.
