Use the Content dashboard to find a planned post, review a generated draft or check a publishing result. Select the correct project, then open **Content > Dashboard**. The selected month and view affect what you see; a dashboard count is a workflow summary, so inspect the post before deciding what to do next.

## The Content dashboard

Use the month arrows to inspect another month and the list or grid buttons to change views. Open a blog from a row or calendar card to reach its detail page. For topics and plan changes, open **Content Plan** in the same sidebar instead.

### Stats bar

**Published**, **Queued** and **Ready** summarize the month-filtered blog records. They keep the same counting basis when you change a list filter.

These cards do not count every record. Posts being written or waiting for images, along with missed, rejected, unpublished and failed posts, fall outside these groups. A post being published counts as Ready even if its displayed label is Generating. Check the list when looking for work that needs attention.

The **Published** list filter uses a post's recorded publication date, while the header continues to use the month-filtered records. The card and filtered list can therefore differ. Neither establishes that a page was indexed or that a downstream website build finished.

### List view

The table shows the date, blog, status and keyword. An additional traffic column appears when connected analytics supplies data for a visible published post. Open a row to inspect its draft or publishing state rather than treating a score badge as a content approval.

Use **All** for the month’s records, **Upcoming** for planned or in-progress work, **Published** for recorded publications and **Failed** for generation or publishing failures. **Rejected** is a separate review decision and is not included in Failed. The Published view shows the recorded publication date; other views use the scheduled date, with the creation date as a fallback when needed.

A behind-schedule notice points to missed posts in the visible list. Review those posts and their current actions before trying to catch up. [Scheduling & Missed Posts](/content-seo/scheduling-missed-posts) explains the difference between refreshing a plan and generating a missed article.

### Calendar view

The grid shows scheduled posts in a Sunday-to-Saturday layout. The current view spans five weeks and shows the first blog for a day. Use the list to inspect additional posts sharing a date or dates outside the displayed grid; do not assume the calendar contains every post in the selected month.

Select a visible card to open the blog. The color and short label help you find it, but the detail page provides the status and actions needed for the next decision.

## Status legend

Display labels group some technical states. **Generating** can cover writing, pending images or publishing; **Ready to Publish** can cover an approved or scheduled post. With an active destination, the list can show **Posting** for that latter group. Check the post rather than interpreting every label as a separate completed step.

A review draft can appear as **Generated** when no active integration is connected. That label does not establish that the content was reviewed or sent to a website. **Rejected**, **Generation Failed**, **Failed** and **Schedule Missed** describe different situations and call for different actions.

Use [Blog Status Lifecycle](/content-seo/blog-status-lifecycle) for the full state reference, and [Draft & Approval Workflow](/content-seo/draft-approval-workflow) when deciding whether a draft is ready. If someone must approve content before publication, check the project's [publishing preset](/content-seo/publishing-modes) separately.

## Per-project quota

Check the selected project's allowance separately from its dashboard counts. **Published**, **Queued** and **Ready** describe workflow records; generation usage and planning headroom measure different things.

**Generate more** depends on planning headroom and other eligibility checks. A missing button does not prove that the allowance is exhausted or that the plan is unlimited. Read the current access or limit message and use [Quota & Limits](/content-seo/quota-and-limits) to interpret the applicable cycle. [Generate More & Bonus Blogs](/content-seo/generate-more-bonus-blogs) covers adding extra planned posts and choosing their dates.

## Generating content

A plan-generation progress panel can appear in list view while topic planning is running. The dashboard checks for eligible generation to start or resume, including saved session progress. A panel or completed planning counter does not establish that every article body has been written.

When returning after an interruption, check the plan and saved post statuses before requesting another run. Use [Content Plans & Keywords](/content-seo/content-plans) for topic planning and [Blog Generation](/content-seo/blog-generation) for the separate writing and image steps.

## Bulk actions

Open **Content Plan** to select eligible rows and use the floating **Regenerate … blog plans** toolbar. Project editors can select planned, missed or rejected rows in that view, but selection does not guarantee that the bulk request accepts every row.

Inspect the result and any skipped or failed slots. The current bulk refresh accepts rows whose topic planning is complete and whose blog status is **To be generated** or **Missed**; a selected Rejected post can need its individual detail-page action instead. A plan refresh changes topic information, not the finished article body, and does not automatically reschedule a missed post. See [Content Plans & Keywords](/content-seo/content-plans) before choosing a broader refresh.

## Per-blog actions

Open a blog to use the action offered for its current state. **Generate Content** is offered for planned, missed or generation-failed posts, subject to access and generation eligibility. Review drafts offer **Approve** and **Reject**; approved posts offer **Publish Now**. A publishing failure offers **Retry Publish**.

Published posts can offer **View Live** when a URL is recorded, **Sync Changes** when edits need sending, and **Unpublish**. Unpublished posts offer **Republish**. Before a retry or sync, inspect the receiving website for an existing or partially updated article. [Publishing Errors & Retries](/content-seo/publishing-errors) explains why a failed response can leave the remote result uncertain.

## Blog detail page

Use the detail page to read the draft, check its title and metadata, inspect pictures and review the available SEO suggestions. **Edit Article** opens the editing workflow when you have edit access. Save useful material before requesting a replacement.

Make a direct edit for a specific correction. Use a focused section or image action when the surrounding draft is useful, then inspect what was saved. Follow [Regenerating Content](/content-seo/regenerating-content) for those controls. Approval records your publishing decision; arrange and record any factual or specialist review separately.
